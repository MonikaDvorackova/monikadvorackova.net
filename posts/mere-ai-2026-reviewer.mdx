## MERe AI 2026 — Toronto, Canada

**Role:** Peer Reviewer

**Workshop:** MERe AI 2026, 1–2 December 2026, Toronto, Canada

I am serving as a reviewer for MERe AI 2026, a workshop focused on moral reasoning in artificial intelligence. The role involves assessing research arguments, methodological choices, and the evidence supporting authors' conclusions.

At roughly the same time, I am on the other side of the process: navigating the discussion and rebuttal phase of my own submission to ICLR 2027.

## Both sides of the review

Writing a review and receiving one are different exercises, but they share a responsibility: to make claims precise enough to examine and criticisms specific enough to act on.

As an author, I want reviewers to distinguish between a genuine methodological limitation and a question that can be resolved by clearer exposition or additional analysis. As a reviewer, I owe authors the same distinction. A useful review should identify what the evidence establishes, what remains uncertain, and which changes would materially strengthen the work.

Working on both sides simultaneously has made that symmetry harder to ignore.

## A note on Reviewer 2

Reviewer 2 is a familiar academic character: demanding, skeptical, and occasionally the reason an author opens a document with some reluctance.

The uncomfortable discovery is that a carefully justified objection can be exactly what a paper needs. I may be developing a little more sympathy for Reviewer 2 — provided the criticism is concrete, fair, and supported.

The point is not to make peer review agreeable. It is to make it useful.

## Research integrity

This note describes my role and my own experience, not the content or outcome of any confidential review. It does not imply acceptance of my ICLR submission or in-person attendance at MERe AI.
